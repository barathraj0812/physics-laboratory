Gravity Slingshot

See how a moving planet redirects a spacecraft and exchanges orbital energy.

Interactive simulation
A gravity assist does not create energy. In an ideal flyby, incoming and outgoing spacecraft speeds relative to the planet are equal far from the encounter, while direction changes. A moving planet can transfer a tiny amount of its orbital energy to or from a spacecraft, changing spacecraft speed in the inertial frame. The planet's energy change is usually negligible because its mass is much larger.

Gravity-assist physics

Newton's law gives mutual acceleration between the bodies. A stationary planet can bend a hyperbolic path, but an ideal conservative encounter does not create a permanent far-field speed increase. With a moving planet, equal incoming/outgoing planet-frame relative speeds can correspond to different inertial spacecraft speeds. The energy exchange is balanced by the planet’s motion. The analytical angle below uses the asymptotic impact parameter approximation; this simulation instead integrates from finite-range initial conditions.
