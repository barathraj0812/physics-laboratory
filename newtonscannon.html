What the experiment shows

Newton’s thought experiment and continuous free fall

Newton imagined firing a projectile horizontally from a sufficiently high mountain. As speed increases, the projectile travels farther while gravity continually bends its path toward the planet. An orbit is continuous free fall: gravity never switches off. Tangential motion combined with inward acceleration can make the object continually miss the surface.

Energy, orbit shape, and escape

Specific orbital energy ε = v²/2 − GM/r distinguishes bound (ε < 0), near-parabolic (ε ≈ 0), and unbound (ε > 0) motion in the ideal two-body model. Bound energy alone does not guarantee a clear orbit: a bound path can intersect the physical planet. This lab reports impact separately. A tangential circular launch has vc = √(GM/r); escape speed is vesc = √(2GM/r), the zero-energy boundary at infinity. An oblique launch changes the radial and tangential components and therefore the path; a high speed alone does not guarantee a stable orbit.

Conservation and model limits

Under ideal central gravity, specific energy and specific angular momentum hₙ = xvy − yvx are conserved. Small drift is numerical. The model neglects atmospheric drag, rotation, nonspherical gravity, other bodies, and the projectile’s effect on the planet. Escape-boundary motion takes unbounded time to reach infinity in the ideal parabolic case. Impact time and location use linear interpolation over the final RK4 step, a timestep-level approximation.
