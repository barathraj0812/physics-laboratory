Model, normal modes, and wave interpretation

Hooke’s law: for a linear spring, the force depends on its extension from equilibrium, F = −kΔℓ. In displacement coordinates, a link between adjacent particles pulls according to their relative displacement, not either absolute position alone. Each particle’s acceleration depends on itself and its neighbors, producing coupled oscillations.

Normal modes: a mode is a collective pattern whose relative amplitudes remain fixed while the chain oscillates at one natural frequency. The page builds the mass matrix M and spring-stiffness matrix K and solves K A = ω² M A numerically. The selected mode is initialized using its computed eigenvector; displayed frequencies are calculated, not preset values.

Energy and damping: kinetic energy is Σ½mᵢvᵢ². Spring potential energy sums ½k(Δu)² over every present spring, including the fixed-wall springs. With zero damping, total energy should remain approximately constant; RK4 time discretization causes drift. Viscous damping applies Fᵢ = −cvᵢ and removes power cΣvᵢ², so energy loss is physical in damped runs.

Propagation: a localized disturbance transfers energy through the discrete chain and can reflect from its ends. A disturbance is a combination of normal modes. For a uniform long chain the dispersion relation is ω(q) = 2√(k/m)|sin(qa/2)|; the finite chain’s boundary conditions select a discrete set of modes, and an arbitrary initial pattern is not one exact traveling wave.

Numerics and limits: the model assumes one-dimensional point masses and linear, massless springs. It does not model material deformation or nonlinear elasticity. A fixed base step with automatic RK4 subdivision resolves the fastest estimated mode; smaller steps generally improve accuracy at additional computation cost. Frame delays are capped so hidden-tab stalls do not trigger a large catch-up jump.
